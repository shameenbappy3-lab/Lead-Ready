How it works

Follow the lead from the inbox to a booked visit.

No jargon. This is what actually happens for your customer.

  1. Step 1

    A lead lands in your email

    Someone fills out your website, replies to an ad, or emails you. Same idea for a leaky pipe, a backyard cleanup, or in-home care.

  2. Step 2

    Their details are pulled out automatically

    Name, phone number, and what they need — lawn mowing, a water heater, weekday help at home — are read from the message so nobody has to type it in by hand.

  3. Step 3

    They get an email and a text right away

    The lead is added to your list. They hear from your business immediately — so they know a real company got the request, not a black hole.

  4. Step 4

    A polite assistant calls them

    A natural-sounding voice calls using their name and the job they asked for. First question: are they free to talk about a visit right now?

  5. Step 5

    Two paths: talk now, or pick a time

    If they’re available, the call is handed to your phone so you can close it yourself. If not, a time is set for later — no phone tag.

  6. Step 6

    You get a clear note if a visit was booked

    After the call, if an appointment was set, a short summary and the time land in your email. You show up prepared — name, job, and when.

Same system, three kinds of work

Here’s a Tuesday for each trade.

A dripping water heater at 11:10am

You’re already on a slab leak. The homeowner emails from your contact form: name, address, “water heater leaking.” They get a text and email in under a minute. The assistant calls: “Are you free to talk about the leak now?” They’re at work, so Thursday 8am is booked. You get the summary before lunch.

Spring cleanup while you’re on a mower

A new neighbor wants hedges and a first cut. You can’t hear the phone over the equipment. The system still greets them, asks if they can talk, and when they say “after 5,” it sets the time. You finish the current yard, then read one email: who, what, and when.

A family looking for weekday help

A daughter writes in about afternoon care for her dad. You’re with another family. She gets a warm text and email immediately. On the call she’s free, so it rings you. You take it in the car and set a meet-and-greet before anyone else calls her back.
